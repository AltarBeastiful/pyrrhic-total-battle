## What the numbers say

- **68 stops** walked; the push *moves* something on **41** of the 68 mercenary readings and **0** of the 68 monster ones. What the pool line *draws* is narrower: see the fourth bullet.
- **Units added**: 1,492 mercenaries over the mercenary pushes, 0 monsters over the monster ones.
- **The kill order was broken on 0 readings** — 0 is the promise (`pushedCounts` bounds every pushed stack strictly under the stack above it).
- **And it is the biggest safe push**: of the 102 stacks the answer leaves standing, **1** could take one more unit without moving the kill order, inside the stock and the housing — `Aydae alone, 4 975 (one captain, four hired types — experiment 103’s camp) · burn-saver · authority · legionary-6 at 10`. The one case is a **tie**: the unit lands on exactly the health of the stack above, which `applyCounts` keeps in place (a tie falls back to the generated order) but the strict bound refuses. The owner's call of 2026-10-12: **the strict line stays** — the plan's own shelter refuses a tie for the same reason ("a tie is not shelter") — so one unit of the 1,492 the benchmark adds is left on the table, and `raise.test.ts` holds both halves of that (`the same net between every health step`, `no stack the push leaves can take one more unit`).
- **The worst opening fell on 3 readings** (mercenaries 3, monsters 0) — the kill order is not the strike order (`engine/battle.ts`). **Read as the app ships it, the button is offered on the 38 readings where it gains and not on those**: the owner read the three of 2026-10-12 and asked for the mark to be hidden where the max would lose damage, which is `offersPush` in `raise.ts`.
- On **22 + 0** readings `Most` would have added more units than the push — the push stops at the neighbour the kill order keeps, and `Most` goes to the troop floor instead.
- **Why the monsters' mark is never drawn**: 18 of the 68 stops *field* a monster stack, and on **none** of them is the mark drawn (the `moved` count above is 0). Of the 169 dominance stacks those marches field, **164 already stand at the line the stack above them draws** (150 of those have a *hired* stack over them — the sizer stands every hired stack at the same ceiling, and a tie there is the engine's own order, which puts the authority pool first), and 126 are at the troop floor's own line. So the monsters have nothing to add **before** the mercenaries' push and, read on the app's own sequence, **nothing after it either (0 of 68 stops)**: the plan rations the mercenaries, because they are a stock, and never a monster (`sustain` is `Infinity` for dominance), so the sizer already stands them where the shelter allows. `pushedCounts` moves a monster the moment a plan or a hand edit leaves one under its line (`raise.test.ts`'s `CHAINED` case: 8 → 9), and `Most` is the position that stands them at the shelter instead — at the price of the kill order. **The pool does move where an army leaves it room**: on the owner's own camp of 2026-10-12 the push moves all four monster stacks on the silver saver — the emerald dragon from 17 units to 21 — for a **4.95 % loss** of worst opening: the strike order, not the kill order (experiment 226, `out/226-why-more-units-can-lose-damage.md`).
