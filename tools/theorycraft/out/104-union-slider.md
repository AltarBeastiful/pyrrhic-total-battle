#### 5 · the marginal reading
A cheaper stop is offered only when **the step up to the next stop is a worse deal than the cheaper stop itself**. Stated exactly, over distinct band marches that are neither a stop nor a stop’s generator, in campaign figures:

1. `C` is **refused outright** when some stop `T` has `silver_T ≤ silver_C`, `damage_T ≥ damage_C` and `burn_T ≤ burn_C` — the bar already dominates it;
2. otherwise `S` is the **nearest stop with `silver_S > silver_C` and `burn_S ≥ burn_C`** (nearest in silver; a tie goes to the lower burn). With no such stop `C` is not a candidate;
3. `C` is offered when `(damage_S − damage_C) / (silver_S − silver_C) < damage_C / silver_C` — the climb to `S` buys damage at less than `C`’s own average rate;
4. among the candidates passing against one `S`, the best damage a silver wins (tie: more damage), and it is placed immediately left of `S`.

## E · assessment

**Why the 2.902 row is not on the bar — two reasons, either of which is enough.**

1. *The bar never saw it.* The reference table is bucketed over **every shape the search prices** (`record`, once per depth × scale inside `evaluateVector`), while the frontier the band, the burn ladder and all five stops are read off keeps only **the strongest shape of each mercenary vector**, plus that vector's tight ladder (`consider`). On his own screen 1 of 11 table rows is backed by a plan the frontier kept. The 3,055,700 / 8,866,762 plan was priced, booked into the table and dropped in the same breath.

2. *It is the extreme the band exists to refuse* — the engine's own verdict on it is **no — one troop stack**. It is **one troop stack** — RD3 265 — carrying 97 hired units. Hired units add damage and are paid for in gold, not silver, so damage a silver is maximised by buying as few troops as the mercenaries can stand behind; the limit of that is a single token stack, which is the owner's own *"the least silver plan would never be chosen … is not a strategy"*. Played for the same horizon it is 8,866,762 damage for 3,055,700 silver against the sweet spot's 16,010,403 for 7,770,800: 55 % of the damage for 39 % of the silver. A player who picked it would not be getting a better plan, he would be getting a smaller one.

So **it is not a defect of the burn axis**. It is the reference table quoting a set the bar is forbidden to draw from: redraw the table over the band and its peak a silver falls from 2.902 to 2.185 — which is what the bar already carries (2.185).

**What the burn axis does cost**, measured over distinct marches:

- the ladder keeps **one march a burn level** (the most damage there), so a cheaper, more efficient march at a level the bar already carries is dropped before any stop is chosen. It happens on 6 of the 12 scenarios, the widest being +9.74 % a silver on "the 4 000-leadership case of 2026-09-15" — always at less damage, so it is a trade and not a free win;
- the bar **runs backwards in silver** on 7 of the 12 scenarios, his own included, because burn and silver are different orders: sweet-spot 7,770,800 / 16,010,403 · steady-max 7,770,800 / 16,976,273 · more-mercs 8,179,400 / 16,360,705 — one step right is more silver and less damage;
- the bar has **no thrift end in silver**: his stops cost 7,770,800–8,179,400, while the band carries campaigns down to 6,851,900;
- and the band's own efficiency peak is **already on the bar** on 10 of the 12 scenarios, so no re-placement over the frontier can buy much efficiency (here the best off-bar march is 2.020 against 2.185 on it).

**Recommendation — two computations, in this order.**

**(a) Draw the reference table from the plans the bar may offer.** `curve` over `candidates` (the band) instead of over every recorded shape — the same set every stop is chosen from. Measured on his screen: the table's peak a silver 2.902 → 2.185, and its cheapest row moves from 2,140,100 to 6,851,900 silver, which is the true news — this army has nothing to sell under 6,851,900. Without it, every rule below still leaves a table on screen promising what the bar cannot deliver.

**(b) Offer a cheaper stop by the marginal reading (rule 5), not by a second axis.** It is the one rule here that is stated entirely in the plan's own figures, adds at most 2 rows per scenario and never invents a row to fill a chord: a march is offered only when the climb from it to the stop on its right buys damage at less than the march's own rate.
