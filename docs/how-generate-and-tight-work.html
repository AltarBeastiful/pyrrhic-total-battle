Pyrrhic, explained

How Pyrrhic picks a march

Pyrrhic helps a Total Battle player decide which soldiers to send against a big monster. It has two thinking steps. Generate builds a handful of good armies to choose from. Tight then nudges the one you picked a little higher. This page shows how both work, with pictures, toy examples and one real account.

1 · The game

The game in one picture

In Total Battle you send an army, called a march, to fight a monster. The army is not one blob. It is a row of stacks: one stack per kind of soldier. 1,540 Archers make one stack. 20 Arbalesters make another.

Think of a school bus with three separate sections of seats. Each kind of soldier can only sit in its own section, and each seat costs a fixed amount. You cannot bring more soldiers than you have seats for.

Three kinds of soldiers

  • Troops (guardsmen and specialists) sit in the leadership section. If they die you can bring them back for silver.
  • Mercenaries sit in the authority section. They are strong, but you only own a few, and some of them are lost for good every time they fight.
  • Monsters of your own sit in the dominance section. Losses cost silver, training time and dragon coins.

Captains and bonuses make everyone stronger

The enemy always hits the biggest stack

Here is the rule that makes the whole puzzle interesting. Each time the monster attacks, it wipes out the stack with the most total health. In between its attacks, every stack still standing strikes back. So a stack that dies late gets more turns to hit. The trick is to put big, cheap troop stacks on top as shields, so the precious mercenaries die last and strike most often.

What it costs

After the fight you pay to put the army back: silver to revive troops (and monsters), gold for the mercenaries, and time in the training queue. The rarest cost is the mercenaries themselves: a mercenary stack loses one unit for every started group of ten, for good. 9 units lose 1. 10 units lose 1. 11 units lose 2. The app calls that number mercs lost, or the burn.

The real battle also has double-damage chances and per-enemy bonuses. The app plans on the unlucky opening where the monster strikes first, with no lucky extras, so the figures here are the "worst case" damage.

2 · The size of the puzzle

A march is a recipe

Write a march down and it is just a list of numbers: how many of each kind. Like a recipe: 3 eggs, 200 g flour, 1 spoon of sugar. On this account a march is a list of 21 numbers.

How many different lists are there? Try a toy first: some kinds of soldier, each taking one seat, and a bus with a few seats. You may leave seats empty.

Now the real account. It has 11 troop kinds for 11,815 leadership seats, 6 mercenary kinds (limited by how many the player owns) for 2,000 authority seats, and 4 monster kinds for 2,000 dominance seats. Counting every list that fits the seats, exactly:

So the app cannot try them all. It needs shortcuts: rules of thumb that throw away silly recipes early, and a fast way to guess how well a recipe would do without fighting the whole battle. That is what Generate is.

3 · Generate

How Generate works

Generate is a pipeline: each step hands a shorter, better list to the next. Tap a step to see what it does.

Toy: the shelter ladder

This is the most important idea in step 3, so here it is on a toy army. One mercenary stack has 100 health in total. The troops must each stand above it, so the monster eats them first. Pyrrhic puts the lowest troop stack 25 % above the mercenaries, and each next troop stack 2 % above the one below, so rounding can never swap them. Then it tries the whole ladder at bigger sizes.

Real: the cloud of candidates

On the real account the search kept candidate marches. Each dot below is one of them, placed by what one march costs and what it deals. A dot is beaten if another dot is at least as good on silver, mercs lost and damage all at once, and better on one. The coloured dots are the ones nothing beats. That set is the frontier.

4 · The bar

The stops on the bar

A player does not want 5,000 choices. Pyrrhic lines the frontier up by mercs lost a march, from thrifty to greedy, and keeps only the levels where losing more mercenaries buys more damage. Then it names a few stops on that line. A bar shows at most five.

Polishing the stops

Before the bar is shown, Generate runs a few clean-up passes. The most important one is the rated re-typing: it keeps the mercenaries exactly as they are and keeps each troop slot's health, but tries other troop kinds in each slot. It keeps a swap only if the damage does not fall and the grade (next section) goes up. A second pass, the rated stops, lets each stop swap itself for a nearby march (same burn, or one group of ten more) when that march grades at least 5 points better and the bar still reads in order.

There are more passes (a "put-back" that re-adds dropped troop kinds, a "fold" that keeps at most five stops, a thrifty extra stop, a troops-only tail for the last march). They follow the same pattern: try a change, keep it only if the grade or the figures say it is better.

5 · The grade

How the app grades a change

Is 10 % more damage worth 5 % more silver? The owner of the account answered that once, and the app stores his answer as exchange rates. Each rate says how many percent of a cost are worth one percent of damage. A bigger rate means that cost matters less.

A grade of 0 means "no better, no worse". The rated re-typing, the rated stops and Tight all use this one formula.

6 · Tight

How Tight works

Generate's search is coarse on purpose. It tries mercenary amounts at a few fractions (all, 70 %, 45 %, 20 %), not every number. So the march it hands over usually leaves free room. Tight is the second step that fills it. In the app it runs on every stop, and "Tight" is what the march shows by default; "As is" is the march exactly as Generate made it.

Tight follows four rules:

  1. Only add, never remove. Each mercenary and monster stack may grow from Generate's count upward. Troops do not move.
  2. Stay sheltered. No stack may grow past the health of the weakest troop stack, or it would be killed before the troops.
  3. No extra mercs lost. The burn may not rise above Generate's. Growing a stack from 41 to 50 is free (still 5 groups of ten). Going to 51 is not.
  4. Pay for the seats. The authority and dominance seats must still fit.

Every allowed combination is a point in a box. Tight fights each candidate with the real battle replay and keeps the one with the best grade against "As is". So Tight can never come out worse than As is: As is is in the box, with a grade of 0.

Notice the dip: damage is not always higher with more units. A bigger stack has more total health, so it can climb past another stack in the kill order and die a turn earlier. That is why Tight has to fight each candidate instead of just adding "as many as fit".

The box, two stacks at a time

With two stacks you can draw the box as a map: one stack across, the other up. Each square is one march, fought for real. The stronger the blue, the better its grade. Hover or tap a square to read it.

When the box is too big to walk

The maps above hold every other stack at Tight's final answer, so they are flat slices of an 8-dimensional box, and the "simple climb" button is an illustration. The real search tries every pair of stacks together, then every single stack, and restarts from many seeds; it only ever accepts a strictly better grade.

7 · A real puzzle

Why two steps can disagree

On 22 October the player noticed something odd when he raised his captain Leonidas from 0 stars to 1 star. A stronger captain should never make things worse. Yet his Merc saver stop got weaker after Tight. Here is what Generate showed him, and what Tight made of it:

The cause is the order of the two steps. Generate chooses first, using only "As is" figures. The Merc saver's rule is: the fewest mercs lost, and among the marches that lose that same number, the one that costs the least silver. (Only marches the bar is allowed to offer count: at least half the damage and half the damage per silver of the plan's best march, and more than one troop stack.) Tight runs after, and only on the one march Generate chose.

At ★1, the plan's frontier held different marches that lose the same 9 mercenaries. The one that won on silver was a weak one. About 110,000 more silver a march (under 3 % more) would have bought a march that hits far harder, and that Tight could raise far higher. Each dot below is one of those marches.

8 · Words

Glossary

March
One army sent to one fight. Written as a count for each kind of soldier.
Stack
All the soldiers of one kind in a march. The monster kills whole stacks.
Leadership, authority, dominance
The three seat sections: troops, mercenaries, monsters.
Mercenary (merc)
A rare, strong unit the player owns a fixed number of. Some are lost for good each fight.
Mercs lost, burn
Mercenaries lost for good in one march: one per started group of ten, per stack.
Shelter
Keeping every mercenary and monster stack below the weakest troop stack in health, so they die last.
Ladder
The troop stacks Generate builds above the mercenaries, each a little bigger than the one below.
Worst opening
The fight where the monster strikes first. The damage figures on this page use it.
Frontier
The candidates no other candidate beats on silver, mercs lost and damage at once.
Stop
One named choice on the bar: Merc saver, Silver saver, Sweet spot, More mercs, Steady max, All in.
Grade (rating)
Damage change in percent, plus each cost saved in percent divided by its exchange rate.
As is
The march exactly as Generate made it.
Tight
The second step: grows mercenary and monster stacks inside the shelter and the burn, and keeps the best grade.
Box
Every combination of stack sizes Tight is allowed to try.
